A Day Without the Internet

Imagine waking up one morning and discovering that the internet is completely offline. You cannot check social media, stream videos, send messages, or look up information for university assignments. For modern students, this unexpected interruption would feel disorienting because digital connectivity has become deeply integrated into everyday learning and daily communication.

Students routinely rely on digital platforms to attend virtual lectures, coordinate group projects, submit coursework online, and access vast academic research databases. However, spending excessive hours online can also fragment attention spans, increase procrastination, and diminish valuable study time. Without digital distractions, students might rediscover the benefits of reading physical books, engaging in meaningful face-to-face conversations, and efficiently clearing pending tasks. While digital technology undeniably enhances educational convenience, establishing a healthy balance between online productivity and offline personal well-being remains essential.

What is Skimming?

Skimming is a fast reading technique that helps us understand the general idea or main message of a text without reading every single word.

How to Skim a Text

  1. Look at the title: It gives you an immediate hint about the topic.
  2. Read the introduction: Understand what the main idea is.
  3. Notice headings and subheadings: They outline main sections.
  4. Read the first sentences: Topic sentences introduce paragraph ideas.
  5. Look for repeated keywords: Keywords highlight key concepts.
  6. Check the conclusion: Reconfirm the overarching message.

Why is Skimming Important?

  • Saves time: Quickly grasp content from long passages.
  • Finds main ideas: Focuses on key takeaways.
  • Supports academic reading: Ideal for research papers and articles.
  • Filters information: Determines whether text is relevant for your purpose.
